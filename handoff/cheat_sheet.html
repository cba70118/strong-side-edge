The Big Board

A draft list for people who do not follow football. It is already in the order you should pick. Take the name at the top that nobody else has taken yet.

How a draft works

  1. Everyone in your league takes turns picking real NFL players, one at a time. Once someone is picked, nobody else can have them.
  2. Those players earn you points all season for the yards they gain and the touchdowns they score. Most points at the end wins.
  3. So when it is your turn, you want the best person still available — which is exactly what this page tells you.

Take two of these early

The gap between the best running back and the twelfth is 145 points — about 9 a week, and the biggest gap at any position. That is why running backs go first. You start two every week, they get hurt more than anyone, and the good ones almost never come free later. Spend early picks here and do not feel clever about waiting.

Take three, mostly early

You start three receivers, more than any other position, so you simply need more of them. Best to twelfth is 108 points. They also stay healthier than running backs, which makes them the safer half of your first few picks.

Get the best one, or wait a long time

This position has a cliff. The top tight end is 72 points clear of the second, and after that the whole group is bunched: fourth to twelfth is only 28 points apart, roughly 1.6 a week. So either take the one at the top early, or ignore the position until late and take whoever is left. The middle is where picks go to die.

Wait. Really.

Quarterbacks put up the biggest raw numbers, which fools people every year. But you only start one, so what matters is the gap to the next one you could have had — and that gap is only 53 points between the best and the twelfth. About 3 a week. Take one in the back half of your draft and spend the early picks where the gaps are four times bigger.

What the little labels mean

grab early Everyone else tends to wait on this person. Our numbers say they are better than that, so take them sooner than you think you need to.

let them go The opposite. People reach for this person earlier than they are worth. Let somebody else use their pick.

they know something we do not The honest one. Our numbers only look at last season, and 41 of these players just changed teams or barely played, so everyone else is probably right and we are behind. Do not fade these on our say-so.

The order is not simply who scores the most points. A quarterback throws for thousands of yards, but only one of them plays for you each week, so what matters is how much better someone is than the next person you could have had at the same spot. That is why quarterbacks sit low.
